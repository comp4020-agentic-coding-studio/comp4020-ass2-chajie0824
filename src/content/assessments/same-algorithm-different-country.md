## The brief

> Pick one specific problem — worker classification, minimum pay, or
> algorithmic transparency — and show how China, Australia and the EU have
> each answered it differently, and why.

Weeks 7–10 gave you the raw material: China's reliance on layered
subcontracting and individual-business-owner registration to avoid an
employment finding; Australia's 2024 "employee-like worker" minimum
standards under the Fair Work Commission; the EU's Platform Work Directive
and its presumption-of-employment mechanism. A weak essay lists all three. A
strong essay picks one axis of comparison and uses it to explain something
that listing them separately wouldn't show — for instance, why a mechanism
that works in one legal tradition doesn't transplant cleanly into another.

Avoid treating any one regime as simply "ahead" of the others. Each is
answering a slightly different question, under different constraints; the
mark rewards showing you understand the constraints, not picking a winner.

## What you submit

A 1200–1600 word essay, sources linked inline (a government determination,
directive text, or reporting is fine — this isn't asking for a literature
review). State your comparison axis in the opening paragraph.
