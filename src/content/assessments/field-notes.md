## The brief

> Six short notes, one per week, each doing one job: take a claim from that
> week's lecture and test it against a specific case.

This is not a summary assignment. "This week we learned about algorithmic
control" earns nothing. "Rosenblat and Stark's account of Uber's ratings
system as a disciplinary device doesn't fully explain Meituan's scoring
penalty for late deliveries, because the penalty is visible to the rider
before the shift starts, not applied after the fact" earns marks — it names
the claim, and it pushes back with a specific, checkable difference.

Weeks 1–6 stay inside the China case study, so your six notes should read as a
sequence: the marker is watching for whether note 6 shows you thinking with
more precision than note 1, not just about a different topic.

## What you submit

A single markdown or PDF document with six numbered sections, one per week,
150–250 words each. Cite what you're responding to — a lecture slide number,
a linked reading, or a specific detail from the case-study weeks — so a
marker can find the claim you're testing.
