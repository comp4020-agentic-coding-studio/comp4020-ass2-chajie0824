# Policies

## Background expectations

This course assumes you can already write and read code; it does not teach
programming from scratch. What it does teach — reading a system's incentives
from the outside, running your own fieldwork, and arguing a policy position
from evidence — gets built up week by week, so the material does not assume
you have done anything like it before.

## Code of conduct

Treat everyone in the room — classmates, tutors, guest speakers — the way you
would want to be treated in a workplace: no put-downs, no talking over
people, no treating a strong opinion about platform labour as licence to be
rude about the people who hold the opposite one. Disagreement is the point of
a lot of this course's material; disrespect is not.

## Academic integrity

Your fieldwork must be your own observations of a real app on a real order —
do not fabricate an ETA reading or invent a workaround you didn't document.
Quoting or summarising the Trapped in the System case, the Fair Work
determinations, or any other source without attribution is treated as a
breach, whether or not it was intentional. When in doubt about how to cite
something, ask rather than guess.

## Communication

Questions about a week's content or an assessment brief go to the teaching
team listed on the [people](/people/) page. If a question affects more than
one person, ask it publicly rather than by email — the answer usually
belongs to everyone in the room. Expect a reply within two working days;
anything urgent enough to need a faster answer is urgent enough to raise in
person at the next tutorial.

## Tutorial attendance

Attendance at your enrolled tutorial isn't marked on its own, but weeks 2–11
each carry a graded checkpoint that is checked live at the start of that
week's tutorial — miss the tutorial and you miss the checkpoint, with no
same-day retake. Turning up prepared is the only way to bank that 2%, so
treat attendance as load-bearing even though it isn't a line item in the
marking scheme.

## Assignment effort

Word and page limits in an assessment brief are ceilings, not targets — a
shorter answer that meets every spec item outscores a longer one that
pads around it. Markers grade what the spec asks for; effort that goes
somewhere the spec doesn't point is effort the rubric can't reward.

## The week 12 redeemable checkpoint

Week 12's tutorial carries one optional redeemable checkpoint, open to
everyone — no form, no reason required. You respond to any earlier week's
checkpoint prompt you like, not necessarily one you missed, and it's checked
live exactly like an ordinary checkpoint. It replaces your lowest-scoring
earlier checkpoint, but only if that checkpoint scored 0%, and only up to
its usual 2 percentage points — it cannot push your checkpoint total above
20%. One checkpoint, once: however many you missed or scored zero on, this
covers at most one of them. If you already know you'll miss a week, treat
week 12 as where you make it up rather than chasing a same-week swap.

## Extensions

Ask before the due time, not after. A short, specific reason is enough — you
do not need to disclose more than you're comfortable with. Extensions are
granted for the assessment as a whole, not per criterion, and only for the
three graded assessments — weekly checkpoints are covered by the week 12
redeemable checkpoint above instead.

## Special consideration

If circumstances outside your control — illness, misadventure, caring
responsibilities — affect your ability to complete an assessment, tell the
teaching team as soon as you reasonably can, ideally before the due date
rather than after. Special consideration is assessed case by case; the usual
outcome is an extension, but where that isn't workable an alternative
arrangement can be agreed instead.

## Appeals

If you think a mark is wrong, raise it informally with whoever marked it
first — most disagreements are a misreading of the rubric on one side or the
other, and get resolved in that conversation. If it isn't resolved that way,
escalate in writing to the course convenor within ten working days of the
mark being released, citing the specific rubric line you're disputing.

## Getting help

Beyond the [communication](#communication) channel above, the
[people](/people/) page lists who to ask about what, and [lectures](/lectures/)
and [tutorials](/sessions/) link back to it whenever a specific person owns
that week's material.
