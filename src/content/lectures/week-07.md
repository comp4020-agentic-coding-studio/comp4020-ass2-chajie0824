## The question that decides almost everything else

"Employee or independent contractor" sounds like a narrow legal technicality,
but minimum pay, injury cover, unfair-dismissal protection and union access
mostly flow from the answer to it, not from any rule written specifically
about platform work. Get the classification right, from the platform's
perspective, and most other protections simply don't apply yet — whatever a
country's labour law otherwise says about employees.

## China's version of the same manoeuvre

Meituan and Ele.me both route riders through layered subcontracting chains,
registering them as individual business owners rather than employees of any
single entity in the chain — so that no one link, examined on paper, looks
like an employer with the obligations that would imply. It's a specific,
documented legal structure, not a metaphor.

## Not a uniquely Chinese problem

Common-law systems spent the better part of a decade litigating almost
exactly this question over contractor status — a different legal mechanism,
but the same underlying manoeuvre: structure the relationship so the
employment finding doesn't attach. Seeing the China case and the common-law
contractor cases as two implementations of one idea, rather than two
unrelated stories, is what this week's moot is actually testing.

## Before the session

Read the assigned explainer on individual-business-owner registration in the delivery
sector and come ready to argue, in a short moot, either side of "this
